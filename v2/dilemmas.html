    The dilemmas of a public servant.

    Public service is rarely a clean choice between right and wrong. More often, it is a quiet negotiation between right and easy, empathy and rule, present pressure and lasting principle.

    Read the dilemmas
    SBy SurajyapathamUpdated 8 Nov 2026 2 min read
    An officer who has never lost sleep over a decision has not yet served at the edge of the public interest.A senior administrator
    Five recurring dilemmas

    Honest stories from inside the system

    These are not problems to be solved once. They are tensions to be managed every day — and the choices accumulate into a career.

    Doing what is right vs. what is easy

    The right answer often requires friction — with a senior, a contractor, a local strongman. The easy answer is a signature. Knowing the difference is craft. Choosing the right one — every time — is character.

    Balancing empathy with rules

    A widow walks in five days past the deadline. The rule says no. The reason for the rule says yes. Public service is the discipline of finding the third answer — that honours both the rule and the reason it exists.

    Handling pressure from many sides

    Political, departmental, media, public, personal. Each one is legitimate from its own vantage. Holding the centre — the citizen's interest — through all of them is the daily test.

    Serving people during crises

    Floods, pandemics, riots, accidents. The job changes shape. Manuals run out. Systems strain. The officers who do this well are the ones who built the relationships before the crisis arrived.

    Staying motivated in a demanding system

    Transfers, frustrating peers, slow change, occasional cynicism. Motivation is not a feeling — it is a practice. Mentors, peer circles, and small wins keep the long fire going.

    A note for citizens

    Why these dilemmas matter to you, too.

    These are not the troubles of officers alone. They are the texture of the system that touches your land, your school, your hospital, your pension.

    When citizens understand the dilemmas, two things happen: the easy answers get harder, and the right ones get a little more support. That is how culture changes — quietly, on both sides.
